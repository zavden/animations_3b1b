It was a dark and stormy night. The wind hammered the shutters of Ashgrove Manor, and the lights flickered twice before steadying. In the library, seven guests waited for a dinner that would never be served, because their host, old Mr. Whitlow, had not come down from his study.

The butler found him at a quarter past eight, slumped over his desk with an untouched cup of tea at his elbow. The window was latched from the inside, the door had been locked, and the only key hung on a ribbon around the dead man's neck.

Inspector Hale arrived an hour later, soaked to the bone and in no mood for riddles. He listened to each guest in turn. The niece had argued with her uncle about money. The doctor had been seen leaving the kitchen with a small brown bottle. The gardener swore he had spent the whole evening in the shed.

Then there was Derek, the quiet secretary, who had typed every one of Mr. Whitlow's letters for twenty years. He spoke softly, answered every question, and kept glancing at the clock on the mantel, as if he were late for something.

Hale walked the corridors until midnight. He measured the window, weighed the key, and sniffed the cold tea. He noted that the clock in the study ran exactly eleven minutes slow, and that someone had recently wound it.

At dawn he gathered everyone in the library. The tea had been poisoned after the door was locked, he explained, which meant the poison had been placed in the cup long before, by someone who knew the old man's habits better than anyone. The slow clock had given that person an alibi.

"Only one of you knew when he would drink it, and only one of you wound that clock," Hale said, turning slowly toward the fireplace. "And therefore, the murderer was Derek!"
